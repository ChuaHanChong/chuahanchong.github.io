I work on **spatial intelligence**, **embodied AI**, and **real2sim2real**, advised by [Prof. Chen Change Loy](https://www.mmlab-ntu.com/person/ccloy/). The question I care about is how an agent builds a spatial representation of the physical world that is good enough to act on, and how to close the gap between what works in simulation and what survives contact with real sensors and real hardware.

I came to this from nine years of building AI systems in industry rather than from a lab. That work spanned adapting vision-language foundation models to specialised visual domains under tight annotation budgets, semi-supervised and self-supervised learning on largely unlabelled data, continual learning for models that had to keep improving after launch, and the serving infrastructure that carried all of it in production. The sim-to-real gap is not an abstract interest for me; it is the failure mode I kept meeting in deployment, and it is not one you can benchmark your way out of.

Before that I completed an MSc in Artificial Intelligence at NTU, with a thesis on self-supervised visual representations for fine-grained maritime vessel recognition, and a BEng in Mechanical Engineering.
